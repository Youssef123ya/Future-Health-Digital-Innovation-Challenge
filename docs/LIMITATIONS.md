# Zill — Assumptions and limitations register

This register lists what Zill assumes, where the evidence stops, and what has been done about each gap. It sits beside
[SCIENCE.md](SCIENCE.md), which gives the models and their verification, and [ARCHITECTURE.md](ARCHITECTURE.md), which
gives the principles. Every entry comes from the code, the tests, the research notes in `docs/research/`, the module
notes in `docs/dev/` (wave 1, wave 2 and the wave 2c notes), [SIMULATION.md](SIMULATION.md) or
[VALIDATION-COHORT.md](VALIDATION-COHORT.md).

**Status values**

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Open** | Not yet resolved. The fix or the decision is still to come. |
| **Mitigated** | A design or code measure reduces the effect. Some residual risk remains. |
| **Accepted** | Known and accepted for the prototype, because it errs in the protective direction. |
| **Sign-off required** | A named human authority must approve it before field use: an occupational physician, legal counsel, a regulator or native-speaker reviewers. |
| **UNVERIFIED** | The source could not be checked. The value is kept and marked in code. |
| **Product decision** | Engineering has set out the options. The product owner has to choose. |

[ENG] marks an engineering choice that no standard fixes. Such choices are always made in the protective direction.
[ASSUMPTION] marks a behaviour parameter of the simulated cohort.

## 2. Models

| ID | Area | Statement | Why it matters | Mitigation | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| M-01 | WBGT (Liljegren) | The model sets ground temperature equal to air temperature and uses a fixed ground albedo of 0.45. Neither depends on the site. | Ground 20 K hotter than air would add about 0.7–0.9 °C to WBGT at noon. Over hot sand or asphalt the model is therefore likely to read low. | A per-site radiant boost of 0.5–2.0 °C [ENG] is applied to the globe, the wick and MRT, in sun and in shade. A site meter reading overrides the model. | Mitigated |
| M-02 | WBGT validation domain | Liljegren validated the model at US Army depots. The sources contain no validation in a Gulf climate. The expected accuracy of ±1 °C is the paper's claim, from its abstract. | Absolute WBGT accuracy at Abu Dhabi sites is unknown. | Reproducing the published values (Lemke & Kjellstrom: rms 0.056 °C) shows the model is implemented correctly, not that it is accurate in the field. `measuredMinusModelC` is logged wherever a meter exists; the scenario data configure meters at KEZAD and Ruwais. A pilot should compare model and meter. | Open |
| M-03 | WBGT, calm air | The model raises any 10 m wind below 0.62 m/s to 0.62 m/s, following thermofeel. Its attribution to KNMI is UNVERIFIED. The original C code applies only a 0.13 m/s floor at 2 m and gives up to +0.67 °C more WBGT in low-wind sun (u10 0.5 m/s). | In calm air, the modelled WBGT can be up to about 0.7 °C lower than the original code. | An optional calm-air upper bound exists: `includeCalmAirBound` (u2 = 0.13 m/s), reported as `wbgtCalmAirC`. It is off by default. | Product decision; UNVERIFIED |
| M-04 | WBGT latent heat | Quirk Q1: the slope of the latent-heat formula has the wrong sign. It is kept for parity with the reference and changes WBGT by −0.17 to +0.04 °C at 35–50 °C. Whether the paper prints the same sign is UNVERIFIED. | A small systematic offset. | At the temperatures that matter, the reference reads slightly high, which is conservative. | Accepted; UNVERIFIED |
| M-05 | Mean radiant temperature | The MRT that `wbgt.ts` provides is that of the 2-inch black globe, an upper bound on a standing person's MRT: about 94 °C against 74 °C in full sun. `indices.ts` now gives PHS a human MRT computed from the radiation components (Thorsson 2007 / Di Napoli 2020, as phs_iso7933.md §10 recommends), with the ground at air temperature plus the site radiant allowance; the globe MRT is used only for WBGT and TWL. | The human MRT depends on the ground-temperature and radiation inputs; with the ground at air temperature it may read low over hot surfaces (M-01). The simulated ground truth uses the same formula family, so the simulation does not test the MRT formula itself (S-03). | The site radiant allowance is added to the human MRT as well [ENG]. The human-MRT code matches an oracle fixture (`fixtures/mrt_human.json`, 1e-6 °C). | Mitigated |
| M-06 | Solar beam split | The direct and diffuse split comes from Liljegren's clearness formula. Which model supplies Open-Meteo's `direct_radiation` for the UAE is UNVERIFIED. | The beam fraction affects the globe temperature. | The Open-Meteo direct/GHI ratio is used only as a sensitivity check and never drives alerts. | Accepted; UNVERIFIED |
| M-07 | PHS population | PHS models an *average* person. D_lim is set to protect 95 % of the exposed population (ISO 7933:2023 Scope). | Individual variation is not represented. | The physiology and symptom channels can only raise the level. Conservative mode and the RAL apply to higher-risk workers. | Accepted |
| M-08 | PHS beyond the limit | The model has no physiological ceiling: t_re reaches 44–49 °C in fixtures AD03 and AD23. Recovery after t_re ≥ 38 °C cannot be validly modelled (ISO 7933:2023 clause 5 WARNING). | A trajectory after the limit, or a "recovered" projection, would mislead. | Limits latch for the rest of the exposure. Trajectories after D_lim are never displayed. Rest never earns back exposure time: work resumes only after a reviewed recovery. With drinking allowed, a reached water-loss limit restarts after that review only once logged drinking brings the net loss to ≤ 2 % of body mass (SCIENCE §2.9; M-22). Before a limit is reached, a near-limit stop is held until an ISO 7933 resume check passes (ARCHITECTURE §5). | Mitigated |
| M-09 | PHS validity range | 2023 validity range: M 56–250 W/m² (pythermalcomfort's reading, UNVERIFIED), clo 0.1–1.0, t_r − t_a ≤ 60 °C. The ISO 8996 "very high" work class (290 W/m²) is outside it. | Projections for very heavy work, or heavy clothing, are extrapolations. | Such inputs are computed and flagged, never returned as NaN. The design lowers projection confidence. The WBGT limit still applies. | Mitigated |
| M-10 | PHS printed 2023 values | The following printed 2023 values are UNVERIFIED: the body-heat constant, f_cl = 1 + 0.28·I_cl, the M range, the meaning of F_r and the Annex D table, and where h_c is evaluated. The 2023 Annex F values come from a pythermalcomfort issue table. A residual of about 0.5 % in water loss is unexplained. | The code may differ slightly from the printed standard. | The differences are documented: at most 3 g and 1 min for the body-heat constant. The 2004 Annex F is reproduced exactly with its own constant. | UNVERIFIED |
| M-11 | PHS clothing | No verified insulation or permeability data were found for FR, SMS or polyolefin coveralls. The engine uses 1.0 clo and i_mst 0.38, the protective end, at the validity limit. Impermeable suits are outside the scope of ISO 7933. | Projections for these ensembles are uncertain. | `phs.verified = false` is meant to lower confidence (design). For vapour-barrier suits PHS is not run and physiological monitoring is advised. Manikin data (ASTM F1291 / F2370) are needed. | Open |
| M-12 | PHS, no free drinking | Two published readings of the drink = 0 rule exist. The engine defaults to the more conservative one (3 % threshold, then × 0.6), whereas the research note recommends 3 % only. In fixture AD15 the dehydration limit is 71.4 min against 119 min. Logged drinks earn no credit while drinking is restricted, and a reached limit ends heat work for the shift. | This may over-restrict workers who are fasting. In the peak-day scenario the fasting persona is blocked from heat work from 07:01 for the rest of the shift (`docs/dev/wave2c-integration.md` §3). | The default is conservative, and the other reading is available (`threshold3PctOnly`). The choice should be confirmed with occupational health (C-06). | Accepted; Sign-off required |
| M-13 | ACGIH clothing | The ACGIH 2026 additions (−1 °C for short sleeves, +1 °C for a hood) were seen only in the CCOHS reproduction of the paywalled booklet. | The CAV values may differ from the printed booklet. | The −1 °C credit is never applied. Ashley & Bernard 2008 independently supports the hood value. | UNVERIFIED |
| M-14 | Metabolic rate | The values in ISO 7243:2017 Annex A and the ISO 8996:2021 ranges come from secondary sources. The engine uses the ISO 8996:2004 classes and ACGIH Table 3 watts per task class. Individual metabolic variation is not captured. | The work rate is the most uncertain input to the WBGT limits and PHS. | Body-mass scaling only goes up. Shared class boundaries go to the higher class. The simulation tests the effect of hidden individual multipliers (SIMULATION §1). | UNVERIFIED; Accepted |
| M-15 | Acclimatisation | Acclimatisation status is inferred from roster data (`daysOnSite`, `daysAwayRecent`, `experiencedInHeat`), not measured. The rule for loss of acclimatisation is an engineering synthesis. | A worker's actual heat tolerance may differ from the inferred status. | The synthesis is stricter than the ADOSH TG table (a test proves this). Unknown history restarts the new-worker ramp. Anyone not fully acclimatised gets the RAL. | Accepted |
| M-16 | TWL zone-B equation | The sweat-efficiency equation as printed is ambiguous. Zill uses the "physical" reading, which is more protective and up to about 63 W/m² below the alternative in dry, windy conditions. Commercial meters may implement the other reading. | Zill's TWL, and so its zone, may differ from a site's TWL meter. | The alternative (`alt_1p8`) is kept behind a flag for comparison. Both readings are in every fixture vector. | Accepted; comparison with meters pending |
| M-17 | TWL population | TWL assumes self-paced, hydrated, acclimatised workers. A lack of acclimatisation "may reduce this [TWL] by up to 25%" (Miller & Bates 2007). | TWL overstates what an unacclimatised worker can sustain. | Unacclimatised workers are excluded from the Medium and High zones. The environment channel applies the RAL. | Mitigated |
| M-18 | TWL against WBGT | TWL is less conservative than WBGT/TLV. Kapanowski (2018) found a 7 % chance of being unsustainable at the threshold, against 1 % for the TLV/REL. Bernard et al. (2022) report a probability of uncompensable heat stress of 0.14–0.22 at the TWL limit. | Relying on TWL alone would under-protect. | The fused level takes the maximum of the WBGT-based environment channel and the TWL-based regulation channel. | Mitigated; adding a TWL margin is a product decision |
| M-19 | TWL reference | No open-source TWL implementation exists apart from the repository oracle. The psychrometer coefficient and saturation formula are fitted, not printed in the paper. The globe size of the Calor meter used in the UAE studies is unknown. The low-wind gaps against AOH 2002 (+5.6 and +7.6 W/m²) and Miller & Bates 2007 (about +7 W/m²) are unexplained. The paper warns that measurement errors can shift TWL by up to 20 W/m²; a 1 °C rise in dry bulb takes 148 to 134 W/m². | Zone assignments near 115 and 140 W/m² are sensitive to input error. | Boundary values go to the more protective zone (R-01). The WBGT channel runs in parallel. | UNVERIFIED |
| M-20 | TWL withdrawal backstops | Brake & Bates withdraw workers when dry bulb exceeds 44 °C or wet bulb exceeds 32 °C. These backstops are not part of ADOSH v4.0 and are reported as flags only. Inland Abu Dhabi often exceeds 44 °C dry bulb. | Hot, dry inland hours may score acceptably on TWL. | The flags are computed. The WBGT environment channel and the midday break still apply. | Product decision |
| M-21 | Simplified indices | The NWS heat index and the BoM WBGT approximation are implemented for communication and comparison only. The BoM formula over-reads by up to about 7 °C at night: 34.47 against 28.24 °C. | Using either for decisions would be wrong in one direction or the other. | Neither drives a level. Both are labelled in code (`bomApproxWbgtNotForAlerts`). | Accepted |
| M-22 | Self-reported intake | The water-loss limit credits logged drinking: limit = max(5 % BM, 3 % BM + 0.8 × Σ hourly-capped logs) (SCIENCE §2.9). The logs are the worker's own entries, and the 40 % replacement that ISO's 5 % limit implies is the engine's reading of ISO 7933, not a printed figure. | Over-reported drinking would let a worker continue past ISO's 5 % sweat-loss limit, and restart after a reached water-loss limit, with less real replacement than assumed. | Credit factor 0.8 [ENG]; hourly cap 1.42 L; no credit without the `checkins` consent or while drinking is restricted; projections assume no further drinking; without logs the limit is exactly ISO 7933 (tested). The reviewed recovery still needs rest, a clear check-in and sign-off. The pilot should compare logged with observed intake. | Open; sign-off required (C-01) |
| M-23 | Projection against the simulated ground truth | In the shadow arms of the simulated cohort the projection had reached its ISO 7933 limit in 49 % of working samples, yet the Gagge ground truth reached core 38.0 °C or 3 % dehydration within 15 min in 8.4 % of them; in every bin the share was 8.4–12.6 % (VALIDATION-COHORT §3.5). The projection is strongly conservative and does not rank when the ground-truth limit comes. | Minutes-to-limit cannot be read as a time-to-event forecast. Its conservatism drives rest prescriptions and part of the work cost. | Claims discipline: it is presented as a screening projection for an average person (SCIENCE §2.9). The size of the PHS-against-Gagge difference at identical inputs has not been measured; that, and field data, are needed before any recalibration. | Open |
| M-24 | Day plan for the strictest member | The planner plans each crew for its strictest member: the NIOSH RAL when any member is not acclimatised or is in conservative mode, and the next-stricter work/rest tier when any member is in conservative mode. In the simulated cohort all 45 crew-days were bound to the RAL (108 of 333 workers), and the plan scheduled 143 of the 399-minute crew job per worker-shift (`docs/dev/work-cost-drivers.md`). | Protective, but it costs every crew member work time and leaves jobs incomplete. Without the binding members the plan would schedule 179 instead of 139 min per worker (analysis only). | The plan names the need to reassign unacclimatised workers (`unacclimatized_reassign`). Per-member plans or reassignment are the options. | Product decision |
| M-25 | Plan bout rounding | A plan block-hour's work bout is ceil(planned work) minutes (`BlockHour.boutMin`), which can exceed the NIOSH/ADOSH allowance by less than 1 minute per partial block-hour. | A small, systematic excess over the allowance. | The device's projection and channels still apply during the bout. Rounding the bout down would be protective. | Open |
| M-26 | Resume after rule stops | The engine holds only projection stops until a resume check passes. Its task-dependent stops (ADOSH zone exclusion of unacclimatised workers, the heat stop band, the acclimatisation budget, the midday break) drop as soon as the worker rests. The world simulation therefore checks the resume task itself before a worker resumes (SIMULATION.md §3.2). | On a real device, a worker could be released to a task that stops them again within a minute. Without the simulator's check, unacclimatised zone worker-hours rose from 7 to 39 in the first v0.2 cohort run (VALIDATION-COHORT §3.8). | Move the check into the engine as a hold for task-dependent stops, like the projection hold. | Open |

## 4. Physiology

| ID | Area | Statement | Why it matters | Mitigation | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P-01 | ECTemp population | ECTemp was developed on young US soldiers. Validations cover athletes, soldiers, some older walkers and US farm and construction workers, with few women and no Gulf migrant workers. Individual bias can reach about ±0.7 °C. | The estimate may be biased for Zill's users. | ECTemp is shown only as a ±0.6 °C band, and only to the worker. It can raise the level but never lower it. Its thresholds are set conservatively low. | Open: needs prospective validation in Gulf workers |
| P-02 | Sensitivity at high temperature | Sensitivity was 48 % for estimates above 39.0 °C and 4 % above 39.5 °C. ECTemp alone flagged 23.3 % of exertional heat illness cases in one cohort. Bias was −0.32 °C above 38.5 °C. | A normal reading is no reassurance. | Physiology never de-escalates and never produces T3. Symptoms override it. The T1 floor is 38.4 °C, or 38.0 °C in conservative mode. | Mitigated (by design) |
| P-03 | Over-estimation | ECTemp over-estimates in some conditions (+0.34 °C in a WARM group; +0.31 °C in a hydrated baseline), and counted more workers above 38.5 °C than an ingested pill did (34 % against 17 %). | False alarms. | Advisory levels only prompt a check-in. The alert text says "check yourself", not "you are overheating". | Accepted |
| P-04 | Wrist PPG accuracy | Mean absolute error ranges from 4.5 to 14 bpm across 10 devices. Intermittent movement degrades accuracy, and arm-intensive work is the worst case. In construction work, bias against a chest strap was 1.6 ± 4.9 bpm. | Heart-rate errors feed straight into ECTemp and PSI. | A per-minute signal-quality gate (40–200 bpm, ≥ 80 % valid samples, max/min ratio < 2.2, flatline rejection) scales the observation noise. The channel becomes unavailable when fewer than 10 of the last 15 minutes are valid. | Mitigated; validation on specific devices needed |
| P-05 | Heart-rate-suppressing states | Beta-blockers, bradycardia, hypothyroidism and pacemakers suppress heart rate. An offset of 15–27 bpm makes the model underestimate steady-state core temperature by 0.39–0.92 °C. | Undeclared medication makes the channel falsely reassuring. | The `hr_suppressing_medication` flag switches off all HR rules. Because physiology never lowers the level, the environment and symptom channels still protect. The flag depends on self-declaration with consent. | Mitigated (depends on declaration) |
| P-06 | Confounders and resting heat | Fever, infection, psychological stress, caffeine and dehydration affect heart rate. The model is built on the coupling between HR and core temperature during exercise. The research note recommends not showing or escalating ECTemp after more than 60 min of rest [ENG]. `physio.ts` has no activity input and does not implement this. | Possible false alarms at rest. Fever cases were missed by aPSI. | Recent illness is a risk flag. Physiology outputs are not diagnostic. The missing rule errs only towards false alarms. | Open |
| P-07 | Patents and licence | ECTemp is covered by US 10,702,165 B2, its sigmoid refinement by US 12,426,839 B2 and aPSI by US 11,564,579 B2. USARIEM states that commercial use needs a licence. UAE or GCC equivalents were not checked. | Commercial deployment may infringe without a licence. | The patents are stated in the TSDoc. aPSI is not used by the channel. | Sign-off required (legal) |
| P-08 | PSI table and signal-quality constants | The Moran 1998 band table was checked only through its reproduction in US 11,564,579 B2. The Orphanidou max/min ratio of 2.2 comes from secondary sources. | Band labels and quality gating may differ from the originals. | Bands are used only for display, and gating errs towards marking minutes invalid. | UNVERIFIED |
| P-09 | PSI baseline | Without a seated pre-work baseline, HR0 falls back to the first 5 usable minutes of the shift. No screen captures a seated baseline yet. | A baseline taken during work would lower PSI. | PSI is one of three T1 triggers, alongside ECTemp and the sustained-HR rule. | Open |
| P-10 | Recovery heart-rate test | The optional Brouha recovery-HR test (P1 > 110 bpm, or P1 − P3 < 10 bpm) is not implemented. | One documented strain check is missing. | — | Open |
| P-11 | Coverage | The physiology channel exists only for workers who consent and have a paired wearable. | Most workers may be protected without it. | Core safety alerting uses only environment and work context (ARCHITECTURE §7). Physiology is an optional upward adjustment. | Accepted |

## 5. Clinical

| ID | Area | Statement | Why it matters | Mitigation | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C-01 | [ENG] clinical thresholds | Every [ENG] value needs sign-off by a UAE-licensed occupational physician, and prospective validation in Gulf outdoor workers, before field use (physio_and_clinical.md §10.13 item 10). The values are: ECTemp floors 38.0/38.4/39.0 °C, ψ threshold 0.80, 30-min warm-up, 5- and 30-min gap rules, 30-min re-check, 60-min episode link, 10-min supervisor escalation, 250 mL / 20-min reminder, and age 50 when age is unknown. | These numbers shape escalation, but no standard fixes them. | Each value is chosen in the protective direction and marked [ENG] in code and in SCIENCE.md. | Sign-off required |
| C-02 | Protocol sources | The triage table and the cooling protocol are decision support for lay rescuers, built [ENG] from ILCOR 2019, TB MED 507, IOC 2021, OSHA and NATA. The full text of ACSM 2023 is paywalled: its numeric cooling targets were not transcribed, and one co-author dissented. The heat section of the AHA/Red Cross 2024 guidelines was not retrieved. | The protocol may differ from current consensus wording. | The protocol favours action: when in doubt, call 998 and cool first. Zill never uses an algorithm to talk anyone out of calling 998. | Sign-off required; UNVERIFIED |
| C-03 | Dependence on self-report | The symptoms channel needs the worker or a buddy to report.
