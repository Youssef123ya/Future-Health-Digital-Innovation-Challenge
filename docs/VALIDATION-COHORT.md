# Zill — Simulated-cohort validation

This is the third evidence class of [`VALIDATION.md`](VALIDATION.md): **simulated cohort**. It asks what the warning
policy *could* change, under stated assumptions, by running a virtual workforce through real 2025 Abu Dhabi weather
with and without Zill. The design is [`SIMULATION.md`](SIMULATION.md) §3–§6. The code is `src/sim/backtest.ts`
(cohort runner), `src/sim/metrics.ts` (metric definitions) and `scripts/validate/cohort.ts` (this document's tables).

> **Projections, not measured outcomes.** Every number below comes from a ground-truth model (Gagge two-node
> thermoregulation with hidden individual traits, `src/sim/truth.ts`) and from behavioural assumptions (compliance,
> self-report, acknowledgement delays). None of it is a measured health outcome, none of it shows that Zill reduces real
> heat illness, and agreement between two models is not validation against people. Only a prospective pilot can test
> real effects ([`PILOT.md`](PILOT.md) §5).

**Headline, v0.3** (medium run, stamp `zill@0.1.0+code.99c7f34e7c0f+inputs.08e6c3972293`; a simulated cohort
projection). In 333 worker-shifts per arm on 45 site-days, the three Zill arms project no excessive, severe or critical
heat strain and no dehydration above 3 %, against 114 excessive-strain worker-shifts per 1,000 under the midday ban
alone [95 % CI 56–184] and 39 [18–63] under the site TWL index. The price is two thirds of the work: 122 against 367
minutes per shift with the wearable (−66.6 %), and 116 in monitored mode (−68.5 %), set mainly by the standards-based
day plan rather than by device alarms (§3.7). Supervisors still acknowledge 3.4 alerts per worker-shift. In shadow mode
an individual alarm warns by the onset in all 38 excessive-strain worker-shifts, but the alarms do not discriminate
(specificity 3 %). Between v0.2 and v0.3 the simulated world itself changed (task windows for every arm, a drinking
floor calibrated to an observed intake), so the baseline arms moved without any change to Zill (§3.9). §3.8 describes
the design flaws the simulation caught between v0.1 and v0.2.

## 1. How to regenerate

| Command | Cohort | Typical run time on the development machine |
|---|---|---|
| `npx tsx scripts/validate/cohort.ts --quick` | 5 sites × 3 days × 1 crew; sweeps on the hot days | about 70 s in v0.1; longer now that every sweep runs (not re-timed) |
| `npx tsx scripts/validate/cohort.ts --medium` | 5 sites × 9 days × 1 crew; sweeps on the hot days | about 17 min (v0.3: 1,860 site-day runs in about 1,037 s; v0.2: 1,260 runs in about 225 s) |
| `npx tsx scripts/validate/cohort.ts --full` | 5 sites × 18 days × 2 crews; sweeps on the hot and median days | 20–30 min (estimate) |

Size flags (`--days=`, `--crews=`, `--sweep=hot,median,mild`, `--seed=`) give a custom cohort; `--base-only` skips the
variants; `--docs-only` re-renders this document from the JSON. `npm run validate` (scripts/validate/run-all.ts) runs
the cohort step with the mode of the `cohort.json` already on disk (or `ZILL_COHORT_MODE`).

The output is `public/data/validation/cohort.json` (schema `zill.validation.cohort/v1`, TypeScript contract
`CohortReport` in `src/sim/backtest.ts` plus `CohortFile` in the script). It is deterministic for a given code and
data stamp: no clock values, one seed for every run, and a seeded bootstrap. Every run is listed with its seed and
the world-simulation stamp (module version + hash of the full configuration); `variants[].overrides` and
`simConfigFor()` rebuild each run's exact `SimConfig`. The tables between the `validate:begin/end` markers are
generated; do not edit them by hand.

## 2. Methods

### 2.1 Cohort and heat strata

- **Days.** Every day of June–September 2025 at the five sites (Musaffah, KEZAD, Ruwais, Al Ain, Saadiyat) is ranked
  by its shift-mean outdoor WBGT (Liljegren, `engine/indices.ts computeSiteHeat` on the ECMWF IFS analysis, hours
  ending 05:00–17:00, site radiant allowance included). The ranking is cut into terciles (hot, median, mild) and days
  are taken from each tercile at evenly spaced ranks, so one day per stratum is the tercile's middle day.
- **Workers.** The synthetic crews of `data/workforce.ts` (workforce seed `zill-workforce-v1`; the hand-built Musaffah
  demo crew is excluded). The same crews work every selected day at their site.
- **Weather.** The virtual workers live in the IFS analysis. The engine sees what a site would have had: the archived
  ECMWF IFS day-1 forecast (Open-Meteo Previous Runs) through the data layer's fail-safe chain, plus a simulated WBGT
  meter at KEZAD and Ruwais (Liljegren sun WBGT on the analysis + N(0, 0.3 °C)).

### 2.2 Arms

| Arm | What protects the worker | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Midday ban only (`ban_only`) | MoHRE midday break, statutory breaks, self-pacing | Reference |
| Site TWL index (`site_index`) | `ban_only` + ADOSH TWL zone work/rest cycles applied crew-wide from the hourly site forecast | Good current practice |
| Zill (no wearable) (`zill_env`) | Personal environment, projection and regulation channels, check-ins, planner, buddy escalation | Closed loop |
| Zill (with wearable) (`zill_full`) | `zill_env` + the physiology channel for consenting wearers (171 of 333 worker-shifts) | Closed loop |
| Zill (monitored mode) (`zill_monitored`) | `zill_full` on a site that opted in to the monitored plan basis: above the WBGT table limit the tables are a screen for workers with valid monitoring, and the worker's own ISO 7933 projection and physiology enforce (ARCHITECTURE §5 "Plan basis") | Closed loop (new in v0.3) |
| Shadow arms (`shadow_zill_env`, `shadow_zill_full`, `shadow_zill_monitored`) | `ban_only` behaviour; the engine runs and alerts but nobody acts on it (the monitored one in the base case only) | Detection metrics |

Since world simulation 1.3.0 every arm works only inside the tasks' time windows, most of which open at 06:00
(SIMULATION.md §3.1). The midday-ban, site-index and shadow arms work each crew's task list in listed order within the
windows. The Zill arms follow the day plan, which plans the crew rate for its acclimatised members, gives members on an
acclimatisation schedule personal caps, and screens work, rest and idle time with ISO 7933 (ARCHITECTURE §5). How
workers respond to the device is in SIMULATION.md §3.2.

### 2.3 Integrity rules

- **Common random numbers.** All arms and variants of a site-day share one seed; the world simulation forks every
  random stream by seed, date and worker, independently of the policy. A shadow arm therefore reproduces `ban_only`'s
  ground truth exactly — the "shadow − ban only" rows of §3.2 must be zero, and they are checked by a unit test.
- **No circularity.** The engine never sees ground-truth core temperature, true metabolic rate, hydration or latent
  illness (SIMULATION.md §1).
- **No tuning.** The sweep values are fixed in `cohortVariants()` before any run, and no parameter was changed after
  seeing results. A variant whose parameters the world simulation cannot apply is reported as not run, never run with
  silently ignored parameters; in v0.3 every defined variant runs (§4). The design changes between v0.1 and v0.2 were
  decided on stated principles before the re-run. Two fixes came after the first re-run: one protective, one a
  labelling fix that changes no routing (§3.8).
- **One calibrated parameter (v0.3).** The ground truth's habitual drinking floor is no longer assumed but fitted to an
  observation: the mean intake of UAE construction workers over a 12-h summer shift, 5.44 L (Bates & Schneider 2008).
  The fit used the midday-ban-only arm alone, never a Zill arm, on a calibration set larger than this cohort (all 200
  hot-tercile site-days of 2025, 3 crews per site), and changed no other parameter (SIMULATION.md §3.3). The floors of
  v0.1 (1.0) and v0.2 (0.4) are kept as sensitivity variants on both sides of it (§4).

### 2.4 Metric definitions

All definitions are TSDoc'd in `src/sim/metrics.ts`. The unit is the **worker-shift** (one worker, one 05:00–17:00
shift).

| Metric | Definition |
|---|---|
| Event rates per 1,000 worker-shifts | Worker-shifts with ≥ 1 ground-truth event of the kind (SIMULATION.md §2.4 table): excessive strain (core ≥ 38.5 °C, ≥ 38.0 °C if unacclimatised, for ≥ 10 min), severe (≥ 39.5 °C), critical (≥ 40 °C or CNS signs), dehydration (> 3 % body mass). A zero count is shown as `0 (≤ u)`, where u is the one-sided 95 % rule-of-three bound 3/n. |
| Heat-symptom episodes | Ground-truth symptom onsets of the heat-exhaustion, cramps or CNS clusters (thirst excluded), per 1,000 worker-shifts. |
| First-aid evacuations | First-aid cases that ended the shift (the policy-neutral cool-first response, then hand-over). |
| Work / rest / water | Means per worker-shift (not per 1,000): work minutes delivered, protective rest minutes (cycle rest, breaks, instructed/enforced rest, first aid, and no-task minutes inside a rest episode the device asked for), water drunk. Idle minutes (no task assigned) make up the rest of the shift. |
| Alarm (individual alarm) | A distinct alert about this worker (`Alert.basis` individual, ARCHITECTURE §6) at HIGH or above, or at STOP or above where stated, at the first time it was individual at that level. An open exposure alert re-labelled individual counts from the re-label; a missing basis counts as individual. Worker and crew alerts both count. An alert version under designed suppression, which nobody was notified of, is not an alarm; a lifted alert counts from the lift (metrics 1.2.0). |
| Exposure advisory | A worker alert at HIGH or above that was exposure-based (site heat or a site rule) at every version, per worker-shift; a crew alert that bundles worker alerts is not counted again. Per crew-shift: distinct exposure alerts at HIGH or above notified to a supervisor. Advisories are not alarms. Versions under designed suppression are not counted (metrics 1.2.0). |
| Suppressed advisory | A worker-scope exposure alert at HIGH or above logged under designed suppression (the active plan already enforces its action, or it is only the WBGT screen of monitored mode) and never notified, per worker-shift (ARCHITECTURE §6). New in v0.3. |
| Supervisor acknowledgements | Notifications to the supervisor that required an acknowledgement, per worker-shift (both bases). New in v0.3. |
| All alarms (v0.1 definition) | Every distinct alert at HIGH or above covering the worker (worker alert, or a crew alert listing them), at the first time it covered them. Kept for comparison with v0.1 (`detectionAllAlarms`, `eventLeadTimeAllAlarms`). |
| Alert burden by basis and recipient | Notifications per worker-shift by the alert's basis when sent and by recipient role, with those requiring an acknowledgement shown separately. |
| Sensitivity (warned by onset) | Positive worker-shifts whose first individual alarm came at or before the event onset ÷ positive worker-shifts. "Any time in shift" drops the timing condition. |
| Specificity | Worker-shifts without the event and without any individual alarm ÷ worker-shifts without the event. |
| PPV (shift) / PPV (alarm) | Positive worker-shifts among warned ones / alarms with the worker's event onset within ±2 h. |
| False alarms per shift | Individual alarms without the worker's event onset within ±2 h, per worker-shift. |
| Lead time | Event onset − first individual alarm, minutes (positive = warned in advance), over positives warned by the onset; median and IQR. Onsets are ground-truth onsets (an excessive-strain event is confirmed 10 min after it starts). |
| Calibration of minutes-to-limit | Shadow arms, every 5th working minute: the engine's projected minutes to the ISO 7933 limit, binned (0 = limit already reached in the projection; > 0–15; 15–45; 45–90; ≥ 90 or beyond the horizon), against the share of samples whose ground truth reaches core ≥ 38.0 °C or dehydration ≥ 3 % within the bin's window, counting only samples whose window fits in the shift. |
| Event lead time (pilot) | PILOT.md §5.4 (a): first individual alarm at HIGH or above → a later symptomatic event in the same shift (a check-in triaged T2+ by `engine/clinical.ts`, or a first-aid case). Log-derived only. The all-alarm version is kept for comparison with v0.1. |
| Adherence | Instructions followed ÷ instructions shown to the worker. |
| Acknowledgements | Alerts acknowledged ÷ alerts that required an acknowledgement; median minutes to acknowledgement. |
| Midday-break compliance | Worker-shifts without sun work inside 12:30–15:00 (15 Jun–15 Sep), MoHRE MR 44/2022. |
| ADOSH zone compliance | Worker-hours with sun work in a forecast Medium/High TWL hour that stay within the zone's work/rest allowance (ADOSH-SF TG v4.0 Table 1 via `regulation.ts regulatoryFloor`; tasks sharing an hour combine as a dose Σ minutes/allowance ≤ 1) and do not put an ADOSH-unacclimatised worker in an excluding zone (1-min tolerance). |
| Paired differences | Arm − `ban_only` (or variant − base case) over the same worker-shifts. |
| Intervals | 95 % percentile intervals from 1,000 bootstrap replicates resampling whole site-days (workers on one site-day share the weather); paired differences resample the same site-days in both arms. |

**Pilot reuse.** Except for the ground-truth block, every metric is computed from the event log (`alert`, `ack`,
`checkin`, `instruction`, `task`, `shift` events of `src/sim/types.ts`), so a pilot can score its app log with the same
functions; with the log-only target "symptomatic event" the detection functions give the PILOT.md §5.4 (a) event lead
time.

`zill@0.1.0+code.99c7f34e7c0f+inputs.08e6c3972293` (back-test 1.3.0, metrics 1.2.0, world simulation 1.4.0, ground
truth 1.2.0; 45 site-days, 333 worker-shifts per arm, 1,860 site-day runs, about 1,037 s on the development machine).
The work-cost figures come from `docs/dev/work-cost-drivers.md`, generated from the same cohort file. The tables above
are rewritten by every run; this prose is not, so check it against them after a re-run. Every number is a simulated
cohort projection, not a measured outcome.

- **The comparator is not harmless.** Under the midday ban alone, 114 per 1,000 worker-shifts reach excessive strain
  [95 % CI 56–184], concentrated on hot-tercile days (261 per 1,000). There are severe and critical cases (8 and 7
  worker-shifts: 24 and 21 per 1,000, too few for precise rates) and 7 first-aid evacuations. 96 per 1,000
  worker-shifts lose more than 3 % of body mass. The midday break is never violated in any arm; the hazard sits outside
  it.
- **Good current practice helps at little cost.** The site-wide ADOSH TWL cycles (`site_index`) remove about two thirds
  of the excessive-strain worker-shifts (−75 per 1,000 [−135, −27]) and every severe case, for 2.4 % less work time
  [−4.3, −0.6]. It is the comparator any Zill benefit has to beat.
- **The three Zill arms project no excessive, severe or critical strain and no dehydration above 3 %** in 333
  worker-shifts each (one-sided 95 % bound ≤ 9 per 1,000). Heat-symptom episodes fall from 237 to 6 per 1,000 (2
  episodes), and one worker-shift in each Zill arm ends with a first-aid evacuation (3 per 1,000). The dehydration
  result rests on a drinking model that does not penalise drinking too much, while Zill workers drink 7.9 L per shift
  against 5.5 L under the ban (see the over-drinking point below).
- **That protection is bought with a large productivity cost.** With the wearable, Zill delivers 122 instead of 367 work
  minutes per 12-hour shift (−66.6 % [−71.8, −61.5]) and prescribes 307 minutes of protective rest; the rest of the
  shift is idle. The cost is about the same in every heat tercile (−69.6 % hot, −65.8 % median, −64.6 % mild). It is
  set by the day plan, not by device alarms (per worker-shift):
  - The crew job is 399 min. The plan schedules 145 min of it per worker (each worker's own layout); of the crew's
    job, 130 min are deferred and 113 left incomplete, because the allowed work rates do not fit the job into the
    shift and the task windows.
  - Execution loses 22.7 of the 145 scheduled minutes, mostly to projection rests (15.2) and device work/rest cycles
    (5.4).
  - NIOSH/ACGIH time-weighted allowances set the planned rate in 122 of 267 task wall-minutes, at a 68 % work share.
    The ISO 7933 screen sets it in 67 wall-minutes, at 23 %, and the rule pack in 32, at 35 %.
  - Per-member plans are in place (new in v0.3): the crew rate is planned for the acclimatised members, and the 63 of
    333 members on an acclimatisation schedule get personal caps (88 scheduled minutes, against 158 for the others).
  - On 8 of the 9 Al Ain crew-days the plan schedules no work at all (`phs_rest_limit`): Al Ain's rest area is shade
    only, and rest alone, from the shift start, reaches the ISO 7933 screen margin before the 05:00–17:00 shift ends,
    so any work would bring a device stop earlier.

  So the cost follows from the standards (NIOSH/ACGIH allowances, ISO 7933 for work, rest and idle time) and from
  planning only inside the shift and the task windows. It is not a bug, and nothing was tuned. How to reduce it, for
  example cooled rest areas or shorter shifts where rest alone reaches the limit, is an open decision (§8).
- **Monitored mode costs work instead of saving it.** `zill_monitored` schedules slightly more work than `zill_full`
  (150 against 145 min) but delivers less: 116 minutes (−68.5 % [−73.7, −63.7] against the ban), with 289 minutes of
  rest. Individual projections hold more often (23.1 against 15.2 min lost per worker-shift), because the monitored
  crew rate sits at the reference worker's ISO 7933 margin, and devices that cannot confirm monitoring cut back to the
  table cycle (8.7 against 5.4 min); the plan cannot know which devices those are, since wearable and consent status
  are private. In return the alarm burden is lower: 2.35 against 2.61 individual alarms at HIGH or above, 0.64 against
  0.90 exposure advisories and 3.02 against 3.39 supervisor acknowledgements per worker-shift, with the same zero
  events. 98 wall-minutes of task block-hours per worker-shift are planned on the monitored basis. In this cohort the
  trade-off is fewer alarms for less work, which is not what the mode was meant to achieve.
- **The wearable adds nothing measurable at this level** (`zill_full` ≈ `zill_env`: 122 and 123 work minutes and the
  same events). The plan and the environment, regulation and projection channels already set most of the day, and the
  physiology channel can only raise the level.
- **Shadow-mode alarms are precautionary, not discriminating.** Counting individual alarms (§2.4), the arm with the
  wearable warns of excessive strain by its onset in all 38 positive worker-shifts at HIGH or above, with a median lead
  time of 128 min, but specificity is 3 %, the PPV per worker-shift 12 % and there are 1.90 false alarms per
  worker-shift. At STOP or above: 97 %, 108 min, specificity 5 %, PPV 12 % and 0.97 false alarms. The monitored-mode
  shadow arm gives the same detection numbers, because monitored mode does not change the channels that raise
  individual alarms. Exposure advisories describe site or crew conditions; they are counted as alert burden, not as
  alarms. Nearly every worker-shift still receives an individual alarm, so the alarms do not single out who will
  develop strain. (The "technical false alarm" of PILOT.md, an escalation traced to a sensor or forecast error, is a
  different definition.)
- **Alert burden remains high in the closed loop.** Per worker-shift under Zill with the wearable: 2.61 individual
  alarms at HIGH or above (0.88 at STOP), 0.90 exposure advisories, 0.40 suppressed advisories, 3.61 alerts needing an
  acknowledgement and 12.47 notifications. Notifications needing an acknowledgement: worker 3.46, supervisor 3.39 (2.88
  of them individual, about 25 per crew-shift), buddy 0.42. Escalations: 417 per 1,000 worker-shifts. Designed
  suppression recorded 336 alerts in this arm, 273 of which were never notified: it removes the advisories that only
  repeat the plan, not the individual alerts that make up most of the supervisor's load.
- **Over-drinking is a model finding, not a benefit.** Zill workers drink 7.95 L per shift (7.63 L in monitored mode)
  against 5.55 L under the midday ban only. Drink reminders now follow heat exposure, so workers idle in hot shade are
  reminded too, at the work rate (250 mL every 20 min), and without check-in consent the device never applies its
  hourly cap. The ground truth has no penalty for over-drinking, so Zill's simulated dehydration benefit is likely
  overstated (§8).
- **The minutes-to-limit projection is strongly conservative against the ground truth** (§3.5). In 53 % of working
  samples it had already reached its ISO 7933 limit, yet the ground truth reached core 38.0 °C or 3 % dehydration
  within 15 minutes in only 6.7 % of them, and the share is 5.9–20.9 % across the bins. SCIENCE §2.9 discusses why: the
  projection is a conservative screen for an average person, not a forecast of when a worker will overheat.
- **Regulatory compliance.** The midday break holds in every arm. ADOSH zone compliance is 100 % under every Zill arm
  (0 unacclimatised worker-hours; 97.8 % and 8 hours in v0.2), 100 % under `site_index` and 46 % under the midday ban
  only (40 unacclimatised worker-hours).

### 3.8 What the simulation caught (v0.1 → v0.2)

A simulation before a pilot is there to find what is wrong with the design while it costs nothing and harms no one. The
v0.1 cohort (stamp `zill@0.1.0+code.5c82cd69d232+inputs.08e6c3972293`, metrics 1.0.0, world simulation 1.0.0) exposed
four design flaws: two in the engine, one between the planner and the engine, and one in the simulator. Each was fixed on
a stated principle (protective direction, plan and monitor agree, fidelity of the simulated behaviour) before the cohort
was re-run, and none was chosen to improve Zill's numbers. The v0.2 re-runs then caught two more problems.

1. **The water-loss limit ignored logged drinking** (engine). v0.1 applied ISO 7933's drink-allowed limit, a cumulative
   water loss of 5 % of body mass, without crediting what the worker drank, and a reached water-loss limit ended heat
   work for the rest of the shift. A worker who drank as the reminders asked could therefore be stopped for the day on
   sweat loss alone. The v0.1 report attributed most of the lost work to the projection reaching its limit and
   latching. (The v0.2 driver analysis, which v0.1 did not have, shows that the day plan now sets most of the cost,
   §3.7.) v0.2: the limit is max(5 % BM, 3 % BM + credited intake); it is exactly ISO 7933 without logs or without
   consent, and unchanged while drinking is restricted. With drinking allowed, a reached water-loss limit can restart
   after the reviewed recovery once the net loss is ≤ 2 % of body mass (ARCHITECTURE §5; derivation in SCIENCE §2.9).
   v0.3 caps the credited limit at 7.5 % of body mass and credits only drinks logged within 15 min of drinking.
2. **No hysteresis, and a resume the device never showed** (engine and simulator). v0.1 had a 10-minute de-escalation
   dwell but no margin. After a projection stop below 15 min, a rested worker's projection rose to 15–16 min, the device
   dropped to HIGH, and the worker was stopped again within a minute (seen in world-simulation traces). The simulator
   hid this with a resume margin that no device had: 25 min to the limit for the scheduled task, re-checked every
   5 min. Workers then rested on the simulator's account while their device showed less than STOP, and when rest could
   not make the scheduled task feasible the worker simply kept waiting: no device message said so. v0.2: the engine
   holds the stop and releases it only when the task to resume projects 25 min (15 + 10, an ANSI/ISA-18.2 deadband);
   it estimates the rest still needed, says "not feasible now" with `rotate_task` when 60 min of rest would not do,
   and shows a work-bout countdown. The simulator's margin is gone (ARCHITECTURE §5; SIMULATION.md §3.2). In the
   scenario runs, the share of worker-minutes in which the simulator kept a worker resting while the device was below
   STOP fell from 9.0 % to 0 on the peak-day scenario (before and after the integration step;
   `docs/dev/wave2c-integration.md` §3).
3. **Plan and monitor disagreed** (planner, engine and simulator). v0.1's planner set work/rest from the NIOSH/ACGIH and
   ADOSH allowances only, and the device's ISO 7933 projection then stopped workers in the middle of planned work. For
   example, the planner allowed 45 min/h of heavy welding in FR coveralls where PHS projected only about 24–43 min to
   the limit from rest (2025-11-26), which produced repeated projection stops even on a mild day
   (`docs/dev/wave2b-summaries.txt`, worldsim notes). The simulator also ran plans in its own layout, which over-worked
   partial windows: a 40-min block at 30 min/h worked 30 min (`docs/dev/wave2c-sim.md`, item 8). v0.2: the planner
   screens every task-hour with the engine's own ISO 7933 projection in the layout it will be executed in; the device
   caps its work minutes at the plan's rate; the simulator executes the plan's block-hour layout and aligns the device's
   work/rest cycles with it. Following the plan at the planned conditions now gives no projection stop for the
   reference worker (`planner.test.ts`). In the v0.2 cohort execution lost 11.5 of the 143 scheduled minutes per
   worker-shift; in v0.3, with personal caps and holds owned by the engine, 22.7 of 145 (§3.7).
4. **Drinks were counted twice** (simulator). In v0.1 a followed reminder added 250 mL on top of the spontaneous drink,
   and a 450 mL/h hot-shift drinking floor also applied during hours of cooled rest. Virtual Zill workers drank 9.6 L per
   day on the peak-day scenario (up to 18.25 L), and two exceeded the 11.36 L/day cap, which the engine's hydration
   channel then raised to HIGH or STOP (`docs/dev/wave2c-sim.md`, item 7). v0.2: a followed reminder is that minute's
   drink, and spontaneous drinking tracks sweat loss above a habitual floor (SIMULATION.md §3.3). The model and its
   parameter were decided before any re-run. Side effect, kept rather than tuned away: the old floor had hidden
   involuntary dehydration in the baseline arms. The midday-ban arm now drinks 3.9 instead of 6.1 L per shift and has
   282 instead of 51 worker-shifts per 1,000 with more than 3 % dehydration. Zill's differences against the ban on
   dehydration and heat-symptom episodes therefore cannot be compared with v0.1.

Caught by the v0.2 re-runs:

5. **Unacclimatised workers cycled in excluding zones.** With the simulator's margin removed, ADOSH-unacclimatised
   workers went stop → rest → resume → stop in TWL zones that exclude them, because the regulation channel's stop drops
   as soon as the worker rests. In the first v0.2 cohort, unacclimatised zone worker-hours rose from 7 to 39 and zone
   compliance fell from 97.8 % to 90.0 %. A rest episode then also waited until the device's own check of the task to
   resume showed no regulation or environment stop: 8 worker-hours and 97.8 % in the final v0.2 run. Wave 2d moved
   this check into the engine (the task hold, ARCHITECTURE §5); in v0.3 Zill has 0 unacclimatised zone-hours (§3.9).
6. **Exposure alerts were re-labelled individual just before they resolved.** During the de-escalation dwell every
   channel can already read safe, and the sticky-individual rule took "no channel explains the level" for a new
   personal cause: 123 of 144 worker alerts on the peak-day scenario. After the fix, which requires a channel that
   actually drives the level, 2. Routing never depends on the label, so no notification changed (ARCHITECTURE §6).

### 3.9 What changed between v0.2 and v0.3

## 7. What the simulation cannot show

- **It cannot show that Zill reduces real heat illness.** Only a prospective pilot can (PILOT.md). With 200 workers the
  expected number of clinic-level heat-illness cases in one summer is roughly 0.6–3, so the pilot uses feasibility
  endpoints (alert lead time, adherence, false alarms, acceptability, data quality), not incidence (SIMULATION.md §6).
- **Behavioural parameters are assumptions.** Compliance, self-report, acknowledgement delays and first-aider arrival
  are [ASSUMPTION] values (`worldsim.ts DEFAULT_SIM_BEHAVIOUR`); §4 shows how much the conclusions move with them, and
  which assumptions the sweeps do not reach.
- **The ground truth is a model.** Gagge's two-node model is a simplification; the hidden-trait distributions, symptom
  hazards and drinking behaviour are [ASSUMPTION] values (`truth.ts TRUTH_PARAMS`). Agreement between two models is not
  validation against people.
- **Small numbers.** Severe and critical events are rare in the virtual workforce; zero counts are reported with their
  rule-of-three upper bound, and detection metrics for a target with few positives are not estimable.
- **One summer, five grid cells.** The weather is the 2025 IFS analysis at five sites; the engine's forecast is the
  archived ECMWF day-1 forecast. Other summers, microclimates and indoor heat are not represented.
- **Simplified behaviour where it matters.** A supervisor's reassignment of a worker whose task is not feasible now is
  not modelled; by default every drink is logged at its true volume (§4 sweeps logging fidelity); and nothing in the
  ground truth penalises drinking too much (SIMULATION.md §3.2–3.3).
- **The drinking floor is calibrated to one observation.** The ground truth reproduces one published mean intake
  (Bates & Schneider 2008) in the midday-ban arm. The dehydration results depend strongly on it (§4: the Zill benefit
  moves from −72 to −288 per 1,000 across the bracketing floors).
- **The schedule model shapes the comparison.** Generated crews have a fixed job shorter than the shift, and every arm
  works only inside the task windows (SIMULATION.md §3.1). The windows changed the baseline definition in v0.3 (§3.9).

## 8. Model findings surfaced by the back-test

These are behaviours of the current engine, planner and world simulation that the back-test makes visible. They are
reported, not tuned away; each needs a decision by the owner of the module concerned. Findings of earlier versions
that later versions resolved are listed at the end.

**Open in v0.3**

1. **The day plan sets most of the work cost.** Zill delivers 66.6 % less work than the midday ban only with the
   wearable (68.5 % in monitored mode), in every heat tercile. The plan schedules 145 of the 399-minute crew job per
   worker-shift; execution loses 22.7 of the scheduled minutes (§3.7; `docs/dev/work-cost-drivers.md`). Per-member
   plans, the main option of v0.2, are now in place (63 of 333 members on personal caps) and floored bouts removed the
   sub-minute overrun, yet work delivered fell from 132 to 122 minutes. The largest new driver is the ISO 7933 screen of
   rest and idle time, which removes all work on 8 of the 9 Al Ain crew-days (item 2); deferred work rose from 38 to
   130 minutes per worker-shift (`docs/dev/wave2d-integration.md`). The standards' allowances (NIOSH/ACGIH,
   the rule pack, ISO 7933) and planning only inside the shift and the task windows remain the drivers. Options for the
   product owner: cooled rest areas (they shorten the rest each hour needs), rotation to cooler tasks, earlier or
   shorter shifts, or crediting logged drinking at plan time within the 7.5 % ceiling. Each is a design decision, not a
   parameter to tune.
2. **Rest alone reaches the ISO 7933 limit at Al Ain** (`phs_rest_limit`). Al Ain's rest area is shade only, and on 8
   of its 9 crew-days rest alone, from the shift start, reaches the screen margin before 17:00 with no drinking credit at
   plan time, so the plan schedules no Zill work at all; the device would stop resting workers at about that time
   anyway. On the shade-only scenario the plan schedules no work either. In this synthetic workforce the loss falls on
   one crew, and with it on the Urdu-speaking group (§6). The mitigations the warning names are a cooled rest area,
   logged drinking within the 7.5 % ceiling, or a shift that ends before the limit time.
3. **Monitored mode delivers less work than the tables, not more** (§3.7): 115.7 against 122.4 minutes per worker-shift,
   although it schedules more (149.7 against 145.1). The monitored crew rate sits at the reference worker's ISO 7933
   margin, so individual projections hold more often (23.1 against 15.2 minutes lost per worker-shift), and devices that
   cannot confirm monitoring cut back to the table cycle (8.7 against 5.4); the plan cannot know which devices those are,
   because wearable and consent status are private. It does lower the alarm burden (3.02 against 3.39 supervisor
   acknowledgements per worker-shift). Whether a site should opt in is therefore an occupational-health decision about
   alarms against work, not a productivity gain.
4. **Over-drinking.** Zill workers drink 7.95 L per shift against 5.55 L under the midday ban only, and 12.2 L per worker
   on the shade-only scenario (`docs/dev/wave2d-integration.md`). Reminders now follow heat exposure, so workers idle in
   hot shade are reminded at the work rate (250 mL every 20 min), and without check-in consent the device computes its
   fluid status on no intakes, so the hourly over-drinking cap never applies. The ground truth does not penalise
   over-drinking, so the simulated dehydration benefit of Zill is likely overstated; the floor variants show it moves by
   a factor of four with one behavioural parameter (§4). An over-hydration metric and a rest-rate reminder are needed.
5. **Alert burden is still high.** Per worker-shift under Zill with the wearable: 3.39 supervisor acknowledgements
   (about 25 per crew-shift; 2.88 of them individual), 2.61 individual alarms at HIGH or above and 417 escalations per
   1,000 worker-shifts. Designed suppression removes 0.40 exposure advisories per worker-shift (336 alerts recorded, 273
   never notified); the individual alerts that make up most of the supervisor's load are untouched. In shadow mode
   nearly every worker-shift receives an individual alarm (specificity 3 %).
6. **The closed-loop lead time is not estimable.** Under Zill only 2 worker-shifts have a symptomatic event, so the
   event lead time (median 0 min) rests on two cases; the shadow arms (29 events, median 189 min with the wearable)
   carry the detection evidence.
7. **The comparator depends on the drinking model.** v0.2's model raised baseline dehydration from 51 to 282 per 1,000
   worker-shifts; v0.3's calibrated floor lowered it to 96. Zill's differences on dehydration and heat-symptom measures
   are therefore not comparable across versions, and they depend on the floor (§3.9, §4).
8. **Evacuations under Zill** are 1 worker-shift per arm (3 per 1,000), against 7 under the midday ban only; too few to
   rank.

**Resolved in v0.3** (§3.9)
- The resume check for rule stops moved from the simulator into the engine (the task hold). Zill's ADOSH zone
  compliance is 100 % with 0 unacclimatised worker-hours (97.8 % and 8 hours in v0.2).
- Idle and resting workers in hot shade get drink reminders: reminders follow heat exposure (ARCHITECTURE §5). The
  over-drinking this exposed is open (item 4).
- The day plan is made per member where the standards differ per member, and plan bouts are floored.
- The schedules no longer favour the baselines in the coolest hour: every arm honours the task windows (a change of the
  baseline definition, SIMULATION.md §3.1).
- The drinking floor is calibrated to an observed intake instead of assumed, and bracketed by the earlier floors.
- Exposure advisories whose action the plan already enforces are logged under designed suppression instead of being
  notified.

**Resolved since v0.1** (§3.8)
- The water-loss limit ignored logged drinking; the projection had no hysteresis; plan and monitor disagreed; the
  simulator counted followed reminders twice.
- The compliance sweep now reaches the assumption that matters: the supervisor follow-through variant runs (§4).
- The `site_index` comparator no longer restarts the zone cycle when the task changes within a clock hour: tasks share
  the hour's budget as a dose (SIMULATION.md §3.1). Its zone compliance is now 100 % (93.9 % in v0.1).
- The ground-truth sweeps run (§4).
